The Keepers of the Tide

Along the coast, the sea is more than a horizon. It is a livelihood, a memory, and a way of understanding time.

StoryCast Editorial About 4 minutes to read
FIELD NOTES · NO. 001

Before the day becomes loud, the coast is already awake. The tide moves in its own time, carrying the sounds of water, boats, and people beginning their work.

For families who live beside the sea, the shoreline is not simply a beautiful view. It is a place of work and learning, a source of food, and a record of changes that can be felt from one season to the next.

Learning to read the water

Experience teaches people to notice small details: the direction of the wind, the colour of the water, and the shape of waves near the shore. These signs help make decisions about when to set out and when it is safer to wait.

Knowledge like this is often shared through conversation and practice. Younger generations learn by watching, asking questions, and spending time alongside people who know the coast well.

A changing shoreline

Coastal communities also face uncertainty. Weather, changing fishing conditions, and pressure on marine environments can affect work and family life. The future of the shoreline depends on listening to the people who know it closely, as well as protecting the ecosystems that sustain it.

The tide will keep moving. The challenge is to make sure that the people and places connected to it can continue to thrive.
